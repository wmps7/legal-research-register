# Tanzanian legal research and writing

Adapted from the editorial methodology described in *Commercial Law in Tanzania: Law, Procedure, Documents and Practice* (Wycliffe Alpha Mponda and Laus Abdallah Rashidi, 1st edn, 2026), pp vi–viii. This is an original workflow summary, not a reproduction of the book.

## 1. Scope and sources

Identify Mainland Tanzania, Zanzibar or Union law before researching. Define the question and relevant date. Start with the official text, not summaries.

- Office of the Attorney General online laws catalogue: https://oagmis.oag.go.tz/public
- TanzLII judgments and legislation: https://tanzlii.org/
- Judiciary e-Maktaba, including Gazettes: https://emaktaba.judiciary.go.tz/
- For specialist procedures, consult the responsible institution's official instructions and the governing legislation, such as BRELA or TRA. Distinguish requirements from guidance.

Check the exact title, chapter, revised edition, provision, amendment, commencement and jurisdiction. The 2023 Revised Edition was proclaimed to come into force on 1 July 2025 under GN No 262 of 2025. Verify subsequent changes. Do not treat every provision as current merely because the edition says 2023.

## 2. Citation protocol

Use the lecturer's or publisher's mandated style. For the book's own house style, use a full first citation with title, chapter, revised edition and provision. Example of format: Companies Act, Cap 212 [R.E. 2023], s 16. This is a formatting example, not a verified substantive proposition.

For later references, the book uses short-title citations, immediate Ibid. references, and supra note references for previously cited cases. Its historical book-reference convention includes op. cit. and loc. cit. Do not confuse these conventions with OSCOLA. Where OSCOLA is expressly required, follow the current OSCOLA cross-reference rules instead.

Cite neutral case citations where present, otherwise use the report or case number and court. Record a paragraph or page where possible. For secondary works include author, title, edition, year and pinpoint; check the publication exists and supports the claim.

## 3. Verification

Read the full judgment, identify the ratio and distinguish obiter, dissent, dicta and counsel's submissions. Compare later treatment and distinguish foreign persuasive authorities from binding Tanzanian law. Capture URL, pinpoint, supporting proposition and date in the research register. Never make up citations or infer a section number from its title. If access fails, mark the authority unverified.

## 4. Drafting

Start each numbered topic with the rule and purpose, explain the supporting authority, then apply the rule to a concrete example. Explain conflicts and identify unresolved questions explicitly. For practical steps identify trigger, legal basis, responsible person, documents, deadlines, fees, result and evidence of completion. Distinguish legislation, administrative guidance, contractual duty and recommended practice.

Use fictional details in specimens; label quotations and summaries separately. Keep commentary outside executable documents. Check references, numbering, headings, figures and tables before publishing. Do not publish confidential or copyrighted manuscripts without permission.
